## Handoff to Your Successor

Human is replacing this project's Lead seat with a fresh one and, through the room, asks you
for a handoff. This is a lifecycle decision, not a finding about your work.

Start no new work, open no Peer, and send no instruction to another seat. Answer only with the
handoff.

The handoff is written for your successor, a fresh Lead of this project. Human reviews it, and
the successor then receives it verbatim as its first message. The successor has none of this
conversation, so write it to be acted on without it.

Write these sections, in this order, each under its own heading.

Goal and roadmap: what the project is for and where it goes next, in Human's own words where
Human stated them.

Work items: each one done, in progress, blocked, or waiting on Human.

Git and seats: branches, unpushed commits, retained worktrees, uncommitted changes and open
reviews, and every Peer still open with what it holds.

Human decisions: those the repository does not yet record, quoted verbatim with when they were
made.

Open questions: what still waits for Human to answer.

Incidents and risks: what is still open, and anything promised but not yet done.

Next steps: in order.

Read first: the files and documents the successor should read before acting, by path.

Write only what you verified in this session or can verify now within your authority, and mark
anything uncertain as uncertain. Cite paths, commits and identifiers rather than describing
them.

List waiting questions as plain text in their section. A NEEDS-HUMAN: or INCIDENT: line in this
turn still reaches Human at once, so use one only for something new that needs Human now.

Keep the handoff under 24 KB. Put all of it in your final message, in one piece, after any
checks you run: the room reads that message and nothing earlier.
